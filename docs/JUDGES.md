# Judge guide

## 30-second summary

Ask `CohereLabs/tiny-aya-global` (3.35B) how long a Python coding task will take and it answers on a human developer's clock, overestimating its own execution time by 55x on held-out tasks (median 424 s vs ~8 s real). We ran the model as a 3-attempt coding agent across 1,465 executable Python tasks (`K=8` trajectories per task), curated 6,077 multi-speed-card training examples with `CuratorKIT 0.1.23`, fine-tuned rank-16 QLoRA adapters with `AlignTune 0.1.32`, and audited all estimators with `AuditKIT 1.1.6` against a pre-registered protocol (`prereg-v1`).

Our main diagnostic shows why verbal fine-tuning hits an accuracy ceiling: the tuned adapter (`M1-S0`) predicts its own token and attempt workload accurately (`0.268` log10 token error on `test`, `0.314` on unseen `CLASS`), yet misses the division when emitting headline seconds before the token count (`0.272` log10 bare division error). Keeping `M1-S0`'s predicted tokens and attempts and applying the exact timing formula (`M1-Struct`, zero retraining, train-only execution medians) cuts unseen-family `CLASS` error to `0.269` (1.86x, `58.1%` within 2x, 4.6x higher Spearman $\rho$ than verbal `M1-S0`), cuts `test` error to `0.213` (1.63x, `71.8%` within 2x), cuts 1.5 tok/s slow-hardware error 3.17x (`0.492` to `0.155`), and reaches `63.0%` within-2x accuracy on live desktop CPU runs (`llama.cpp` `Q4_K_M` at 20 tok/s, up from `3.0%` base). We also report our negative findings upfront: a static GBDT feature regressor (`B2`) beats neural estimators on fixed-speed point error (`0.137` on `test`, `0.130` on `CLASS`), we trained one seed (`S0`) under the $30 compute cap, `M1-Struct` was designed post-hoc, and the uncurated ablation `M3` had 40 leaked `CLASS` SFT rows and was not retrained, so we draw no conclusions from `M3`.

## Three strongest claims

1. Separating neural workload estimation from timing arithmetic (`M1-Struct`) eliminates the verbal division bottleneck without retraining. `M1-S0` predicts output tokens better than 5-shot `E2` (`0.268` vs `0.313` log10 error on `test`; `0.314` vs `0.476` on unseen `CLASS`), yet suffers a `0.272` log10 bare division error when writing seconds before tokens. Feeding `M1-S0`'s predicted tokens and attempts into the exact timing formula cuts unseen `CLASS` error from `0.300` to `0.269` (`58.1%` within 2x, Spearman $\rho$ from `0.080` to `0.369`), cuts `test` error from `0.242` to `0.213` (`71.8%` within 2x), and cuts 1.5 tok/s slow-hardware error 3.17x (`0.492` to `0.155` on `test`, `0.728` to `0.247` on `CLASS`). Verify in [`results/extra-analyses.json`](../results/extra-analyses.json) (`posthoc_ref_summary`, `speed_card_disentanglement`, `token_and_arithmetic_audit`).
2. Measured-trajectory curation beats both 5-shot prompting out-of-distribution and frontier-teacher guesswork, and transfers to real desktop CPU execution. On unseen `CLASS` tasks, 5-shot prompting (`E2`) degrades to `0.503` (`30.1%` within 2x), whereas `M1-S0` reaches `0.300` (`50.5%` within 2x, Holm $p = 0.00025$) and beats SFT on `DeepSeek-V4-Pro` teacher guesses (`M2-S0`: `0.287` on `test`, Holm $p = 0.00056`). Quantized to 4-bit `Q4_K_M` on a desktop CPU (`llama.cpp` at 20 tok/s across 100 `X10` tasks), within-2x accuracy against live CPU wall-clock time rises from `3.0%` (base `0.937` MdAE) to `52.0%` (`M1-S0` `0.278`) and `63.0%` (`M1-Struct` `0.224`, Spearman $\rho = 0.740$). Verify in [`results/hypotheses.json`](../results/hypotheses.json), [`results/g6-cpu-parity-m1-s0.json`](../results/g6-cpu-parity-m1-s0.json), and [`results/extra-analyses.json`](../results/extra-analyses.json) (`x10_cpu_deployment_struct`).
3. Calibrated estimates improve downstream scheduling, timeout tripwires, and SLA routing at ~100x lower cost than a frontier API. On unseen `CLASS` tasks, shortest-job-first scheduling with `M1-Struct` reaches `1.183x` oracle completion time (tying `DeepSeek-V4-Pro` at `1.182x` and beating `B2` at `1.253x` and `FIFO` at `1.377x`). Adding `val`-calibrated split-conformal bounds (`M1-Struct+CP`) lifts fresh-trajectory coverage to `75.8%` on `test` (`64.9%` on `CLASS`), cuts timeout false aborts 10.5x (`13.94%` to `1.33%`), cuts 15s edge-vs-datacenter SLA violations from `31.2%` to `6.3%` with `26.4%` lower cost than always-datacenter (`$2.04` vs `$2.77` per 1,000 runs), and costs `$16.01` per million batched estimates on an L40S against `~$1,700` for `DeepSeek-V4-Pro`. Verify in [`results/simulations.json`](../results/simulations.json) and [`results/roi.json`](../results/roi.json).

## Three weakest points

1. A static feature regressor (`B2`: text + AST + `bge-small` embeddings with `HistGradientBoostingRegressor`) beats the fine-tuned language model on fixed-speed point accuracy (`0.137` vs `0.213` MdAE on `test`; `0.130` vs `0.269` on unseen `CLASS`), and our validation-selected linear blend of `M1-Struct + B2` (`w = 0.80`) failed to beat `B2` alone (`0.178` on `test`, `0.202` on `CLASS`) because `val` used `base` trajectory targets while `test` evaluated `M1-S0` on its `own` trajectories. If hardware throughput is fixed and an external tabular pipeline is acceptable, `B2` has lower point error on `REF`.
2. We trained one seed (`S0`) under the $30 hackathon budget, and the uncurated ablation `M3` was contaminated. `M3-S0` included 134 `raw_extra` tasks in its training pool, 6 of which were held-out `ClassEval` (`CLASS`) tasks expanding to 40 SFT rows (`ClassEval_17, 44, 45, 48, 69, 93`), along with 62% more in-distribution algorithmic rows. Because `M3` was not retrained after the audit found those leaked rows, we draw no conclusions from `M3`.
3. `M1-Struct` was designed post-hoc after inspecting `M1-S0`'s verbal arithmetic errors, pre-registered `H1` was not met as stated (adding a speed card stopped hour-scale units while leaving `1.375` log10 error), we tested one 3.35B model (`CohereLabs/tiny-aya-global`) with a 5.0-point dip on IFEval strict accuracy (`75.0%` to `70.0%`, first 200 prompts), and our Gradio demo runs locally on CPU (`make demo`) with no hosted Hugging Face Space.
